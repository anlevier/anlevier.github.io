### About Me

> “If meaning lies even partially in usage, then you subtly alter the language every time you use it. You couldn't leave it intact if you tried.”

Brian Christian, a poet degreed in both computer science and philosophy, spoke about the fluidity of language in his book [The Most Human Human: What Talking with Computers Teaches Us About What It Means to Be Alive](https://brianchristian.org/the-most-human-human/). In his book, Christian writes about entering the Loebner Prize, arguably the most notable Turing test. In the competition some of the world’s most advanced AIs are judged on a series of five-minute conversations in the hopes of being voted The Most Human Computer. Christian enters the competition in the hopes of achieving the competition’s lesser-known prize - The Most Human Human Award.

His quote highlights a shared passion for the intersection of language and computer science. More importantly, this quote exemplifies my personal philosophy: if everything we touch is changed by our interaction and also changes us, we should strive to leave things better than we found them, take only what serves us, and move continuously forward.

### Education History

I graduated with a Bachelor’s Degree in English from McNeese State University. At McNeese I cultivated my love for language in my studies and first as a member, then later as President, of the Speech & Debate Team. I entered the professional workforce as an Instructional Designer before shifting to marketing web development. This small shift made me desire more technically challenging work and I received a scholarship through Girl Develop It to [complete a Ruby on Rails bootcamp with Bloc.io](https://www.themarysue.com/interview-girl-develop-it/).

After working in the software engineering industry for eight years I was still curious and closely watching AI's impact on the field. Ever the student, I enrolled in [The University of Arizona's M.S. in Human Language Technology program](https://linguistics.arizona.edu/ms-hlt), which I completed in 2026. In my graduate program, I’ve focused on NLP, syntax, and translation, completing advanced coursework in statistical NLP, computational linguistics, and speech technology. My academic and professional journeys reflect the same theme: curiosity, adaptability, and a love for language both human and machine.

### Education Application and Future Goals

My goal now is to apply my industry experience while contributing to meaningful, people-first technology. I bring sharp front-end skills, clean and testable full-stack code, and an eye for elegant UX. I’ve built production features across various stacks as well as operationalized LLM pipelines. My recent projects include human text-span annotation, multi-stage quality and depth sentiment classification, and sentiment and relevance labeling. 

I have worked across the NLP stack using skills in statistical information retrieval and text classification, sequence labeling, and classical language tasks using machine learning. I also have experience using neural methods with PyTorch and Hugging Face transformers.

As an engineer I care about writing maintainable code and leaving things better for the next dev. Just as importantly, I’m a compassionate communicator who uplifts team culture. Feel free to get in touch!
